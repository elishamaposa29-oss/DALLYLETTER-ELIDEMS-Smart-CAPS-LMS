# Dallyletter Elidems — Social launch identity

## Brand position
**Dallyletter Elidems** is an ambitious learning and education-technology platform with Zimbabwe and South Africa as its initial geographic focus. It is designed to support diverse African educational syllabuses and welcomes learners, educators, schools and educational organisations worldwide.

**Founder and sole creator:** Elisha Maposa.

Do not describe the platform as government-accredited, nationally certified, or as fully supporting every African syllabus unless those claims are independently verified and the relevant curriculum coverage is actually live.

## Core message
**Learning without borders. African educational innovation.**

Supporting line: **Zimbabwe first, with South Africa and the wider African region also welcomed. African curricula in view. Open to the world.**

## Audience
- Learners seeking accessible digital learning support.
- Teachers and educators who want digital tools for teaching and learner engagement.
- Schools and educational organisations exploring digital learning and administration.
- International collaborators interested in African education technology.

## Brand voice
- Ambitious, credible, clear and welcoming.
- Proudly African without suggesting that the platform is limited to Africa.
- Evidence-led: describe live features accurately; label planned capabilities as in development.
- Avoid unsupported claims about user numbers, school partnerships, accreditation, results or curriculum coverage.

## Visual direction
- Primary navy: #0f2a5a
- Accent gold: #b38b00
- Supporting palette: white, warm off-white and restrained charcoal.
- Use the existing Dallyletter Elidems logo and interface assets; do not invent partner logos or institutional seals.
- Prefer clean layouts, strong typography, real product screenshots and real classroom/learning imagery with appropriate permission.

## Content pillars
1. Product progress — show real, working features and explain what they solve.
2. Learning support — practical study habits, learner guidance and responsible digital learning.
3. Educator enablement — demonstrate tools that help teachers prepare, teach and engage learners.
4. African curriculum context — discuss Zimbabwean and South African pathways and progressively document syllabus coverage.
5. Founder journey — share the building process transparently, with Elisha Maposa clearly credited as founder and sole creator.
6. Community listening — ask learners, educators and schools what they need; do not imply endorsements from respondents.

## 30-day publishing rhythm
- Week 1: Brand introduction, founder story, mission and geographic focus.
- Week 2: Short demonstrations of verified, working learner features.
- Week 3: Educator and school use cases; invite feedback rather than claiming partnerships.
- Week 4: Product progress recap, roadmap priorities and a clear invitation for pilot conversations.

Recommended cadence: 3 useful posts per week, plus replies to genuine comments. Prioritise quality and consistency over daily promotional repetition.
